aboutFont=Psila Grotesk is a reinterpretation of an unnamed woodcut font stored in a drawer in the letterpress workshop at the Academy of Arts, Architecture and Design in Prague, labeled only as “úzký grotesk”. The typeface is narrow, bold, geometric, sans serif, and is characterized by curved diagonals and high minuscules. It is comparable to well-known fonts such as Inserat-Grotesk or Steinschriften from the late 19th to the 20th century. Such fonts appear on book and record covers, posters, magazines, etc., as headline fonts, but also on a few food store signs in Prague. In general, these fonts—as well as “úzký grotesk”—have a quite modern look, but also some old-fashioned vibes that could be dusted off in order to make them fit for more timeless and contemporary uses.
